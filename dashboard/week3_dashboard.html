A preliminary power-rating engine for Week 3

Built on the composite of six public models (FPI, nfelo, Inpredictable, Unexpected Points, FTN DVOA, PFF), decomposed into a real offense/defense split (SIS DataHub run & pass defense data — no longer a synthetic 50/50 heuristic) and run through a 5,000-iteration Monte Carlo per game, layered with a QB-level adjustment from confirmed Week 3 starters (SIS DataHub, 2025 season + 2026 through Week 2), then shrunk toward the market line at a weight calibrated on Week 2's validated results. Treat this as a v0.4 baseline — see improvement levers for what would materially sharpen it before staking real money on it.

Team power ratings

01  /  RATINGS

Offense/defense are now a real split, not a synthetic heuristic: defense comes from SIS DataHub's team run-defense and pass-defense points-above-average (2025 season blended with 2026 through Week 2, weighted for roster turnover), and offense is the residual of the vendor's own identity — composite = offense − defense — so it needs no separate offense data at all. Std Dev is the spread across the six input models — it becomes each team's simulation-variance input, not a ranking factor. What's different about this build

Two structural fixes landed this week, both aimed at the exact gaps Week 2 exposed: offense/defense is now a real split from SIS DataHub run & pass defense data instead of a synthetic 50/50 heuristic, and the total-points formula was rewritten to actually use both teams' defense (it previously summed the two offenses in isolation, which is likely why totals ran so low). Full injury report is also now integrated (backup-QB flags in §03); non-QB injuries are parsed and referenced but not yet a calibrated point adjustment — see §06.

What actually happened

Week 2 was upset-heavy: Carolina routed a Cooper-Rush-led Atlanta 34–3 (the model had that game near a coin flip), Cleveland won outright at Tampa Bay as 8.5-point underdogs, Las Vegas won outright at the Chargers as 6.5-point underdogs, and New Orleans came back to beat Baltimore on the road. None of those are visible in a pre-season composite rating with no injury or in-game context — but the closing market didn't fully see them coming either, and it still out-performed both model versions on every measure here.

What this says about the model

Straight-up accuracy matched the model's own confidence (a coin-flip-heavy slate should land near 60–65%), but margin error and total-points error were both worse than the textbook priors this build leaned on — and worse than just taking the closing line. That's the whole reason for the market blend feeding this week's numbers: shrink toward the market by a weight that grows as validated weeks accumulate (currently , since Week 2 is the only week scored so far). Blended against Week 2 itself, MAE drops to 11.84pt and straight-up accuracy rises to 11/16, matching the market — a calibration check, not an out-of-sample validation, since the blend weight and the market itself both came from this same slate; Week 3 is the real test. The clearest, most falsifiable lesson: point totals ran systematically low on the week's shootouts (Bills–Lions, Chiefs–Colts, Commanders–Cowboys all landed 15–30 points over the model's total) — traced to a real bug (total points never used either team's defense) rather than just a missing-data gap, and fixed this week alongside the real off/def split (§01).

Injury report

06  /  AVAILABILITY

Full-league report (all 32 teams, per user instruction: Questionable = will play, Doubtful/Out/Injured Reserve = won't), filtered to positions that move a line — QB, RB, WR, TE, OT/OG/C, CB. RB/WR/TE entries are now labeled with a depth-chart tier (e.g. WR1, RB2) ranked by real 2025 season usage (targets for WR/TE, rush attempts + targets for RB) instead of a bare position — role, not performance: a low-efficiency workhorse still ranks above a highly-efficient backup, which is deliberate, since PAA (points above average per opportunity) reflects how good a player was, not how big his role was, and using it to rank "starter vs. backup" would mislabel exactly the players — an inefficient lead back, a struggling WR1 — this section most needs to get right. Hover a chip for the source; a * means the tier is estimated from the player's 2025 usage on a different team (a trade or free-agent signing since). This is still a reference list, not a calibrated point adjustment: converting "their WR1 is out" into a spread shift needs a games-played denominator these files don't include, so a per-game point value isn't in the simulation yet (lever list below). QB-position entries here are already reflected in §03's starters.
