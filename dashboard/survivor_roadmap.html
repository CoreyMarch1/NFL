Methodology

Where the win probabilities come from

Every future matchup is priced with a 50/50 blend of two views: this week's composite power rating (the FPI/nfelo/Inpredictable/Unexpected Points/DVOA/PFF blend already built and validated for the weekly matchup model) and each team's 2026 season win total, taken from a September sportsbook consensus. The composite is precise but reflects a single week's form; the win total is a market's full-season read but doesn't update play by play. Blending them is a deliberate hedge against trusting either one alone this far in advance. Week 3 is the exception: it uses the fully-built weekly model directly — real market lines, the QB adjustment, and the injury point-adjustment — since that's strictly more information than the blend has for any other week.

Why this isn't just "pick the biggest favorite each week"

Survivor is a resource-allocation problem: using a great team in an easy week you didn't need them for is the same mistake as saving a mediocre team for a week you did. This roadmap treats the whole remaining season as one optimization — a weeks×teams assignment problem solved to maximize the product of all 16 win probabilities at once, not the sum of each week's best pick taken greedily. That's why a team can show up as a strong "alternative" in one week's detail panel while the plan actually uses them several weeks later: the math found a better overall path by saving them.

The load-bearing assumption: this plan is only as good as "the season plays out roughly as expected." A season is long — injuries, coaching changes, and bad weeks will move some of these probabilities before they matter. Full-season plans, revisited weekly, beat locking in and forgetting it.

What this can't see yet

  • Weekly injury reports, starter changes, and weather for any week past the current one — the same real-time signal the main weekly model uses for Week 3 doesn't exist yet for Week 4 onward.
  • Market line movement between now and kickoff, which often carries sharp information (injuries, weather) before it's public.
  • Opponent quality drift — a team's composite rating is this week's snapshot, held static for every future week rather than re-derived as the season actually unfolds.

Re-run model/survivor_model.py weekly as the composite ratings and injury picture update — the plan will re-optimize the remaining weeks around whatever's actually true by then.
