A preliminary power-rating engine for Week 2

Built on the composite of six public models (FPI, nfelo, Inpredictable, Unexpected Points, FTN DVOA, PFF), decomposed into offense/defense splits and run through a 5,000-iteration Monte Carlo per game, then layered with a QB-level adjustment from confirmed Week 2 starters (SIS DataHub, 2025 season + 2026 Week 1). Treat this as a v0.2 baseline — see improvement levers for what would materially sharpen it before staking real money on it.

QB report: who's actually starting

03  /  STARTERS

Combines passing and rushing production (SIS DataHub points-above-average, both facets) into one per-game value, blending each 2026 Week 2 starter's full 2025 season form with their 2026 Week 1 game (recency-weighted, capped by sample size), then feeds a small, capped points-per-game adjustment back into the simulation as partial regression toward the established baseline — a QB running hotter than his track record gets nudged down for Week 2, a QB running colder gets nudged up. Rushing matters here: Patrick Mahomes' Week 1 line looks far worse on passing alone (−3.6 pts) than combined with his scrambling (−1.6 pts), and Jalen Hurts' and Lamar Jackson's ground games turn a mediocre passing week into a net-positive one. Every one of this week's 32 starters also started Week 1, so the published composite ratings already reflect each team's actual Week 2 arm — the one gap that adjustment can't close is the three teams starting a QB clearly below their normal QB1, marked Backup below.

✓ Actual result fell inside the 68% CI

Reading this correctly

One game is not a validation set — it's one draw from a distribution that's 13–15 points wide by design. The market (BUF −5.5) was closer to the eventual margin than the model's +4.7, which is the expected pattern this early: Vegas prices injuries, weather, and sharp money the composite doesn't see. Across the other 15 games, though, the model's median spread lands within 1 point of the market line on 12 of them — the composite-plus-HFA approach tracks consensus closely even without those inputs; see the per-game "edge" callouts below for where it diverges.

Score-wise, the model projected BUF 26.7–DET 22.3 (total 49.0) against an actual 41–31 (total 72) — both teams well over the model's total, which the off/def split (built on a 22.5-pt league-average baseline, not this specific matchup's pace) had no way to anticipate.
