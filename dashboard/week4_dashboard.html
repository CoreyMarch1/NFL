Team power ratings

01  /  RATINGS

Net rating is the @SamHoppen Week 4 composite with one input refreshed: its Inpredictable column is swapped for Inpredictable's Oct 4 betting-market GPF and the six-source average recomputed. Inpredictable is already one of the six sources, so it's refreshed in place rather than added as a seventh, which would double-count it. Each team moves by a sixth of its Inpredictable change, at most ~0.3 pts. Off/Def now come from Inpredictable's market-derived dGPF (defense = league average − dGPF; offense is the residual of composite = offense − defense). This replaces the SIS split that was stuck at Week 2 data. Against the 11 real Week 4 market totals, that old split missed by 4.6 pts on average, worse than no split at all (4.1); dGPF misses by 1.0. Since dGPF is itself built from betting markets, that agreement is partly circular. It shows consistency with the market, not proven accuracy, and actual Week 4 totals are the real test. The split only feeds projected totals; spreads come from the composite. Std Dev is the spread across the six input models — it becomes each team's simulation-variance input, not a ranking factor. Trend is computed directly from the Week 3→4 change in the published composite (like-for-like, before the Inpredictable refresh) (direction + a magnitude bucket at ±1.0 / ±2.0 rating points).

Validation

05  /  VALIDATION

Week 4 is final (scores via user-supplied scoreboard screenshots), scored against the projections exactly as they stood before kickoff. The headline: on the 11 games with a market line, the model was about even with the closing line — slightly lower margin error, the same straight-up record. Across both out-of-sample weeks (Weeks 3–4, 26 lined games) the model and the market are effectively tied. That's a respectable result for a composite-rating model, but not an edge, and 26 games is far too few to tell the two apart.

The miss everyone shared

Falcons 45, Saints 24 · 23.5 points of margin error

Model: NO −2.5 · Market: NO −2.5 · Actual: ATL +21

The single biggest miss was one the market made too — same line, same blowout. The other large errors split: Colts 30–13 in London (no line sourced; the model had Indianapolis by just 2.3) and Giants 36–24 over Arizona (the model and market both had the Cardinals). Meanwhile Rams–Eagles, Packers–Bucs, and Chargers–Seahawks all landed within 0.2 pts of the model's margin.

The real test of the off/def switch

Week 4 moved the offense/defense split from stale SIS data to Inpredictable's market-derived dGPF, because dGPF matched the market's totals far better (1.0 vs. 4.6 pts) — a partly circular check. Against actual totals the gap is much smaller: dGPF missed by 9.5 pts on average across all 15 games, the SIS split by 10.1, and no split at all by 10.7. So the switch helped, but modestly — and on the 11 lined games the market's own totals (9.95) still beat it (10.46). Every split also ran ~2–2.5 pts low: Week 4 scored more than anyone projected (Falcons–Saints 69, Cowboys–Texans 64, Cardinals–Giants 60).

Weeks 3–4 combined (26 games with a line)

Margin error: market-blended model 7.31, unblended QB+injury model 7.18, closing market 7.40 pts. Straight-up: 14–15 of 26 for every model stage vs. 14 of 26 for the market. Week 3's story was two blowouts (Bears 27–7, Jaguars 35–6) the model had the right side of but nowhere near the size of; Week 4's was a high-scoring slate and one shared miss in New Orleans. Notably, blending toward the market made the model slightly worse this week (7.55 vs. 7.15 unblended on the lined games) after helping in Week 3 — noise at this sample size, not a reason to retune.

Where this leaves the model

Three validated weeks (46 games) now: market-competitive on margin and straight-up picks, no demonstrated edge, and still prone to large misses on blowouts no composite rating can see coming. Week 4 itself used VALIDATED_GAMES = 31; the Week 5 build should use 46, which moves the market-blend weight to 46/(46+64) ≈ 42% model / 58% market. Totals remain the weakest output — worth a dedicated look (a league-scoring baseline above 22.5, or blending in the market total the way margins already blend in the spread).

Injury report

06  /  AVAILABILITY

Carried forward unchanged from the Week 3 report — not reconfirmed for Week 4, so any injury news since then (returns, new injuries) isn't reflected below. Full-league report (all 32 teams, per user instruction: Questionable = will play, Doubtful/Out/Injured Reserve = won't), filtered to positions that move a line — QB, RB, WR, TE, OT, OG, C, CB, S, LB, DE, DT. Every non-QB entry is labeled with a depth-chart tier (e.g. WR1, DE2) ranked by real 2025 season usage — targets for WR/TE, rush attempts + targets for RB, snaps for OT/OG/C, and combined run-defense/pass-rush/coverage snaps for CB/S/LB/DE/DT — instead of a bare position. Role, not performance: a low-efficiency workhorse still ranks above a highly-efficient backup, which is deliberate, since PAA (points above average per opportunity) reflects how good a player was, not how big his role was, and using it to rank "starter vs. backup" would mislabel exactly the players — an inefficient lead back, a struggling WR1 — this section most needs to get right. Hover a chip for the source; a * means the tier is estimated from the player's 2025 usage on a different team (a trade or free-agent signing since). This feeds a real point adjustment in the simulation (see §04's matchup notes and the levers list below for the method and its caps) — each tagged injury is priced against the next healthy player at that spot using 2026-to-date points-per-game. QB-position entries here are already reflected in §03's starters.
